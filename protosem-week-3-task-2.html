01Overview

Placing parts together is the easy part. Making them behave like a real product is where engineering starts. In Fusion 360 I modeled a 1 L steel bottle with a joint-driven cap, and animated a pulley that opens into an exploded view and returns to its original position.

Before this, I thought assembly in CAD just meant placing parts together, and I was honestly unsure how joints and storyboards would work.

02Concepts

Components

Each part is its own body, so the bottle and cap can move independently once assembled.

Joints & DOF

A joint separates placing two parts together from defining how they behave, and sets how many degrees of freedom they have.

Boundary Fill

Fills an enclosed space between selected bodies. The node chosen inside the bottle tells Fusion which space to fill.

Storyboards & timeline

Animation workspace storyboards place each move on a timeline, so parts can start at different times.

06What I Learned

01Assembly is only the start

A joint defines how parts behave and how many degrees of freedom they have, and that is what makes an assembly work like a real product.

02Joints control motion

The bottle and cap taught me how joints control motion: the cap only felt real once it could spin, lift and return.

03Timing controls order

The pulley taught me how storyboards and timing control the order in which parts move.

04Show how it works

An assembly has to show how the parts fit together and how they are meant to move. I want to use this on future mechanisms like gears and pulleys.
